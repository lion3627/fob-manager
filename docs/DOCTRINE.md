| In the simulation | Practice it follows |
| --- | --- |
| A raid is organised into an assault element, a support element and a security element (here the vehicle element at the drop, and in the platoon raid a blocking squad) | Raid organisation by task (general knowledge, unchecked) |

## Operations: known departures and simplifications

- **Special Forces team.** Twelve, as an ODA, split six assault, three support with a machine gun, three drivers. Real raid forces are often larger and include attachments.
- **Seats.** Vehicles carry any number of passengers. Real seating would need more vehicles for the platoon.
- **Platoon headquarters.** The platoon leader, sergeant and radio operator are not modelled; the command post talks to each squad directly.
- **Lift, not shift.** The support element stops firing when the assault reports secure; it does not shift fire to depth, and there is no fratricide.
- **Assault.** The assault creeps in silently until compromised, then moves by rushes (twenty seconds moving, ten down and firing). No breaching, no room clearing; the compound is a point.
- **Capture.** A target who is wounded, or alone or suppressed with an assaulter within 25 m, surrenders. A wounded target near a blocking or support position is collected once the shooting stops nearby.
- **Search.** Fifteen minutes on the objective.
- **Internal net.** Elements within 5 km and line of sight hear each other's reports at once; the command post gets them through the normal link.
- **Manpack satellite radio.** Usable after a minute halted, 30 to 90 seconds delay. Real TACSAT voice is faster but needs the antenna up; the numbers are placeholders.
- **Enemy alertness.** An unalerted guard at night notices far less (a factor of 0.15 on detection). Engines are heard 1.4 km away at night, 0.9 km by day; gunfire 3 km. All tuned by feel.
- **The target's choice.** Woken by gunfire, the target hides in the compound about a third of the time and otherwise slips out after one to four minutes at night.
- **Time cap.** An operation ends eight hours after H-hour if not everyone is back.

General knowledge, unchecked. To verify against ATP 3-21.8 (room clearing, breaching) and FM 3-06, *Urban Operations*.

| Assault teams, a perimeter (security) team with an interpreter, and an air element that inserts, holds off the objective and returns for the pickup | Raid organisation for a helicopter-borne assault force (general knowledge) |

## Compound raid: known departures and simplifications

- **Walls block sight, not movement.** Teams walk the routes the plan gives them, through doorways; nothing stops a man from walking through a wall except that nobody plans to. Soldiers in file step where the man in front stepped.
- **Two floors.** Sight, fire and hearing across floors: sight and fire never cross a floor, hearing always does. One staircase.
- **Rooms.** Five rooms, each a rectangle; furniture, windows and roof access are not modelled. The yards are not cleared.
- **Close-quarters fire.** Inside 20 m with an aimed track, rate of fire goes up fourfold. Hit chance goes up sixfold for US shooters and two and a half times for the occupants, who have no night vision and were asleep: tuned by feel against the real-world record of such raids.
- **Waking.** Sleepers wake on helicopters (1.2 km at night), breaching charges (2.5 to 3.5 km), gunfire, a hard landing, or manual breaching close by, and are armed 15 to 45 seconds after a blast or gunfire, 45 to 120 seconds after other noise. One of the three men is awake now and then.
- **Collateral.** A round that misses a man at close range has a 2% chance of hitting someone within 1.5 m of him. Charges wound people within 3.5 m on the other side 20% of the time.
- **Hard landing.** Landing in a walled yard gives a 15% chance that the second aircraft lands hard and cannot fly; its crew go with the perimeter team, and the remaining aircraft carries everyone home (seats are not counted). There is no backup aircraft.
- **Interpreter.** Part of the perimeter team; crowd control and tactical questioning are not modelled, and no one from the town comes to look yet.
- **Ground force commander.** Not a person. The teams decide on the internal net; the command post can call ALPHA (breach now), DELTA (exfil now) or abort, and each reaches the teams by radio with the usual delay.
- **Search.** Seven minutes per team after its last room, with no materials modelled.

## Known departures and simplifications

- **Staff titles.** S3 and S4 are battalion staff officers. A company-sized base would be run by a commander, executive officer, first sergeant and supply sergeant. The titles were kept because the brief asked for an operations officer and a logistics officer; the cleanest fix is to make the base a small battalion task force command post, or to rename the roles.
- **Platoon structure.** There is no weapons squad, and the platoon leader and platoon sergeant exist only as people at the command post.
- **Vehicle crews.** Each squad rides in two vehicles with a driver and gunner each; the other five dismount. Real load plans vary by vehicle.
- **Machine-gun rates.** The M249 fires at about 50 rounds a minute, which matches the commonly quoted sustained rate. The M240 rate of about 100 a minute is from general knowledge and is unchecked.
- **Position beacons.** Vehicles report position every five minutes and can pass text over satellite in two to five minutes. Real update rates and latency vary; these numbers are placeholders.
- **Maintenance.** The service interval is compressed to about 110 km so that maintenance competes with operations inside a few days. Real scheduled services are far less frequent; daily checks are not modelled.
- **Fuel quality.** Tanks are sampled once a day by rule. Real fuel surveillance has several tests on different schedules.
- **Rations.** Only MREs, with a three-year shelf life. The expiry mechanism works and is tested, but nothing perishable is in the scenario.
- **Medical evacuation.** There is no air MEDEVAC; units carry their wounded back to the aid station. Time-to-surgery does not yet affect survival.
- **Route clearance and bomb disposal** are not modelled. Convoys are escorted but nobody clears the route.
- **Engineers.** The base is already built. The arrive, build and depart cycle for engineers described in the design document is not in this slice.
- **Fires and aviation.** No mortars, artillery, attack aviation or unmanned aircraft.
- **The enemy** is a generic irregular force with rifles, a machine gun and a rocket launcher per cell. Its behaviour follows no specific doctrine.
- **Combat resolution** is statistical per round fired. Hit probabilities were tuned so that a few minutes of fighting between a squad and a cell produces a handful of casualties, not calibrated against data.
- **Night fighting.** Friendly fire at night is 0.85 as accurate as by day, enemy fire 0.5, standing for night vision devices on one side only. This applies to the living base as well as to operations.
